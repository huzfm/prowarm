Most of India needs heating for a few weeks a year. The Himalayan belt needs it for five months — and that single fact reorders every design decision we make. A system that's perfect for a Gurugram bathroom would be an expensive mistake in Manali. Here's how the mountains change the maths, drawn from a decade of projects between Srinagar and Gangtok.

## Duty cycle decides the fuel

At a 100-hour heating season, hardware cost dominates and electric wins. At 2,500 hours, running cost dominates everything. That's why practically every whole-home system we install above 1,500 metres is hydronic, and increasingly heat-pump driven.

Modern air-source heat pumps hold useful output down to −15 °C, and a radiant floor is their ideal partner: because the emitter is the entire floor, the water only needs to reach 35–40 °C — exactly the range where heat pumps are most efficient. The same machine feeding wall radiators would need 60 °C water and twice the electricity.

## Thermal mass is a feature, not a bug

In plains bathrooms we fight for fast response. In the mountains, we want the opposite: a 65 mm heated screed that charges up during the day — ideally on daytime solar generation — and radiates through the evening. Several of our Himachal projects run the heat pump almost exclusively in daylight hours and coast to midnight on the slab's stored warmth.

> A screeded floor is a battery you walk on. In the mountains we size it deliberately, like any other component.

## Details that only matter when it's −8 outside

Cold-climate design is mostly a list of small disciplines:

- **Insulation under and around.** Perimeter strips matter as much as under-slab boards; an uninsulated slab edge bleeds heat straight into frozen ground.
- **Glycol dosing** for any pipe run through unheated spaces, checked at annual service.
- **Room-by-room balancing.** A south-facing room with mountain sun needs a fraction of the flow of a north bedroom; flow meters on the manifold make the difference visible.
- **Backup heat source.** We pipe manifolds so a small gas boiler can step in during a heat pump service — redundancy is not optional at altitude.

## The Cedar House numbers

Our longest-running hotel installation — 22 rooms in Manali — has completed two full winters on an AquaFlow screed system with twin air-source heat pumps. Season-long metering puts heating cost at **₹41 per room-night**, roughly a third of the diesel-boiler estimate the owners were originally quoted elsewhere, with zero guest complaints about cold floors in either season.
