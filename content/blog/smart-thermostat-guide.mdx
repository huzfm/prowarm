A heated floor with a badly configured thermostat is like a fine car in second gear — it works, but you're paying for capability you never feel. Ten minutes of setup separates the two. This is the configuration our support team applies on every commissioning visit.

## Schedule the floor, not the clock

The classic mistake is scheduling heating the way older generations set geysers: on at 6, off at 10, regardless of who's home. Think instead about *when feet touch floor*:

- **Bathroom** — 45 minutes before the first shower, off once the morning rush ends. Evening boost if your household bathes at night.
- **Living room** — from an hour before you typically gather until bedtime. The floor's stored heat covers the stragglers.
- **Bedroom** — warm for the hour before sleep, then *off*. A heated floor under a sleeping human is wasted comfort; blankets are free.

The Pro's learning mode will refine these windows automatically after a couple of weeks, trimming run-time from the edges of each slot.

## Comfort vs eco: the two temperatures that matter

Set your **comfort** temperature to the lowest value that feels genuinely warm — for most people that's 24–26 °C floor temperature, not the 30 °C new owners try first. Then set **eco** (setback) around 5 °C lower rather than fully off; recovering a mildly cool floor costs less than reheating a cold one within the same evening.

> Every degree of floor temperature you don't use costs roughly 7% in energy. The difference between 28 °C and 25 °C is invisible to your feet and visible on your bill.

## Turn geofencing on — properly

Geofencing switches the system to eco when the last phone leaves the radius, and starts recovery when the first one heads home. Two settings make or break it: add *every* adult's phone (one missing phone means heating an empty house), and set the radius to 2–3 km so recovery begins during your commute, not at the gate.

## Wooden floors: check the limit

If you have engineered wood or laminate, open **Settings → Floor → Max temperature** and confirm it reads **27 °C**. Our installers set it at commissioning, but a factory reset restores the tile default of 32 °C, and your floor's warranty depends on the lower figure. This one setting is why every ProWarm wood installation ships with a floor probe rather than air-only sensing.

## Read the monthly report

The SenseWarm app's energy report converts run-time into rupees at your tariff. Glance at it once a month: a room whose cost creeps up without a weather excuse usually means a door left open, a rug added over the sensor zone, or a schedule that no longer matches life. Thirty seconds of attention keeps the system honest.
