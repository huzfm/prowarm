The most common question we hear on a first call is also the simplest: *what will it cost me?* The answer depends on three choices — system type, floor area and how you'll run it — but none of them needs to be a mystery. Here is the arithmetic we walk every customer through.

## The three numbers that matter

Every underfloor heating quote is really three numbers stacked together: hardware, installation and running cost. Get all three before comparing suppliers, because a cheap mat with an expensive habit is no bargain.

### Hardware

Electric heating mats for a typical bathroom start around **₹1,150–1,650 per square metre**, depending on whether you need a self-adhesive mat for tile or a foil system for laminate. Water-based systems cost more upfront — roughly ₹1,700–2,100 per square metre — but repay the difference in running cost if you're heating a whole home all winter.

### Installation

For electric systems, installation is usually 30–40% of hardware cost, including the thermostat wiring and a levelling pour. Hydronic systems need a manifold, a heat source connection and balancing, so budget closer to 60%.

### Running cost

This is where insulation quietly decides everything.

> An insulated bathroom floor in Delhi costs about the same to run per evening as a ceiling fan does in summer. An uninsulated one can cost double.

On a metered test in our Gurugram demo home, a 4 m² insulated bathroom floor drew roughly 0.6 units per evening of typical use — under ₹6 at current tariffs. Skipping the ₹380/m² insulation board pushed that past ₹11. Over five winters, the "saving" on insulation costs you many times over.

| Bathroom | 4 m² | StickyMat 200W | ₹14,000–18,000 | ₹150–250 |
| Bedroom | 12 m² | WarmFoil under laminate | ₹36,000–48,000 | ₹400–700 |
| Whole home | 120 m² | AquaBoard hydronic + heat pump | ₹4.2–5.5 lakh | ₹2,500–4,000 |

Prices assume insulation boards under every system — which we consider non-negotiable — and Delhi-NCR electricity tariffs.

## Where people overspend

Two mistakes inflate budgets needlessly. The first is heating floor you never stand on: under wardrobes, beds and kitchen islands. A good layout drawing removes 15–20% of the heated area, and the cost with it. The second is buying a whole-home electric system for a cold-climate house that runs heating six months a year — at that duty cycle, hydronic wins decisively.
