Underfloor heating comes in two families. **Electric** systems warm the floor with a resistive cable or foil element; **hydronic** (water-based) systems circulate warm water through pipes buried in or under the floor. Both produce the same lovely result — gentle, even, invisible warmth — but they suit very different projects.

## Where electric wins

Electric systems are thin, fast and cheap to install. A self-adhesive mat adds 3 mm to the floor build-up, fits around a renovation without touching door heights, and needs nothing but a wire back to the thermostat.

- **Single rooms and renovations.** A bathroom refit is the classic case: two days of work, one circuit, done.
- **Fast response.** Tile over an insulated electric mat is warm in 15–25 minutes — perfect for rooms used in short bursts.
- **Zero maintenance.** No boiler, no pump, no annual service. There is simply nothing to go wrong.

The trade-off is running cost. Electricity is a premium fuel, so heating large areas for many hours daily gets expensive.

## Where hydronic wins

A water-based system costs more to install — pipes, a manifold, a heat source — but produces each unit of heat far more cheaply, especially from a heat pump.

- **Whole homes in cold climates.** If your house is in Shimla, Srinagar or Dehradun and heats for five months a year, hydronic running costs are typically 50–65% lower.
- **New construction.** Pipe cast into the screed turns the slab itself into a radiator, at the lowest cost per square metre of any configuration.
- **Thermal mass.** A screeded hydronic floor holds warmth for hours after the heat source stops — cosy at 2 a.m. without drawing a watt.

> Our rule of thumb: under 20 m², or heating a few hours a day — go electric. Whole homes that heat all season — go hydronic.

## The honest comparison

| | Electric | Hydronic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Installed cost | Lower | Higher |
| Running cost | Higher | Lower |
| Build-up height | 1–5 mm | 18–75 mm |
| Heat-up speed | 15–25 min | 45–90 min |
| Best for | Rooms, retrofits | Whole homes, new builds |
| Maintenance | None | Annual check |

## You can mix them

The comparison isn't either/or. Many of our projects run hydronic through the living areas and bedrooms, with a small electric mat in an upstairs bathroom where extending pipework would be disruptive. Each zone gets the system that suits it, all behind the same SenseWarm controls.
