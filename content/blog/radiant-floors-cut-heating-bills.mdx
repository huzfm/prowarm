Every winter, the same scene plays out in millions of Indian homes: a blower heater roaring in the corner, the air near the ceiling stifling, and everyone's feet still cold on the stone floor. The 40% saving radiant floors deliver isn't marketing arithmetic  it comes from three specific inefficiencies that warm-air heating can't escape.

## Heat rises. You don't live at the ceiling.

A convection heater works by making hot air, and hot air's first instinct is to leave you. It stratifies: 30 °C at the ceiling, 18 °C at ankle height. You're paying to heat the two metres of air above your head.

A heated floor inverts the gradient. The warmest layer of the room is the one your body actually touches, and temperature falls gently with height. Nothing is wasted on the ceiling.

## Warm feet change what "warm enough" means

This is the part that surprises people. Human comfort tracks skin contact and radiant exchange far more than air temperature.

> With warm floors underfoot, most people report full comfort with air 3–4 °C cooler than they'd demand from a blower.

Each degree you *don't* heat the air saves roughly 7–10% of energy input. Drop the setpoint from 26 °C to 23 °C because your feet are warm, and you've banked a 20–30% saving before any other factor counts.

## No fan, no leaks, no re-heating

The remaining slice comes from mundane engineering:

- **No fan** running for hours (a blower's fan alone draws 30–50 W).
- **Zoned control**  the SenseWarm thermostat heats the bathroom for the morning rush and the living room for the evening, instead of one appliance overheating one room while the rest stay cold.
- **Thermal mass**  a heated slab keeps releasing warmth long after the element switches off. Duty cycles of 30–40% are typical once a room is at temperature.

## The measured result

Across 214 metered ProWarm installations we surveyed in the 2025–26 season, homes that replaced blower or oil-column heaters as their primary heat source reported **31–42% lower winter electricity consumption** for equivalent comfort hours  with insulated systems clustering at the top of that range.

The single biggest variable? Insulation boards under the heater. [We've written before](/blog/underfloor-heating-cost-india-2026) about why the cheapest component in the system controls the most expensive number on the bill.
